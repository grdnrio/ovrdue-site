Three circles. One tap.

Most apps for keeping in touch turn your friends into a to-do list. Ovrdue does less, on purpose.

1

Pick your people

Choose the friends you actually want to stay close to and put them in a circle: inner, good, or catch-up. Each circle has its own rhythm, from every couple of weeks to a few times a year.

2

Ovrdue keeps count

The Mac helper notices when you last spoke to each friend on WhatsApp, iMessage, phone and FaceTime, and when you last met up in your calendar. It only ever looks at dates, never what you said.

3

Reach out in a tap

When someone's gone quiet, their dot turns red. One tap opens the chat. Send the message, and Ovrdue marks it done. A weekly nudge, nothing more.

Your friendships, at a glance

Every friend is a dot on the ring for their circle. Green means you're in touch. Red means it's been too long. Open the app and you know who to message before you've finished your coffee.

It syncs between your iPhone and Mac through iCloud, so the helper does the noticing and the phone does the reaching out.

Works with how you actually talk

Ovrdue picks up the last time you spoke from the places conversations really happen.

WhatsApp iMessage and SMS Phone and FaceTime Calendar meet-ups Instagram Messenger One-tap logging for everything else

WhatsApp, iMessage and calls are read by the Mac helper. Instagram and Messenger come from the data export Meta lets you download. Anything else, log it with a tap.

Your data stays on your devices

A friendship app that uploads your messages to a server would be a strange thing to trust. So Ovrdue doesn't have one.

  • Only dates are read. Message text is never opened, stored or sent anywhere.
  • No account to create. Sync between your iPhone and Mac runs through your own iCloud.
  • Nothing leaves your devices. We can't see your friends, and we don't want to.
  • Ovrdue suggests, it never assumes. You confirm who counts as a friend.

Questions

Isn't this just a personal CRM?

Those exist, and most of them feel like doing admin on your friends. Ovrdue has no pipeline, no notes you're meant to keep up, no scoring. It answers one question: who have I not spoken to in too long?

Does it read my messages?

No. The Mac helper reads when a message was sent or received, and who with. It never reads what was said, and nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Why does it need a Mac?

iPhone apps aren't allowed to see your WhatsApp or iMessage history. A Mac that's signed into the same accounts can. The helper sits in your menu bar, does the noticing, and syncs the dates to your phone through iCloud. The iPhone app still works on its own if you log catch-ups by hand.

Will there be an Android or Windows version?

Not at launch. Ovrdue is built on iCloud sync and on what a Mac is allowed to read, so it's an Apple thing for now.
